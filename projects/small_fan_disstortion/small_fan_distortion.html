Project Overview & Objectives

Designed an aerodynamic contraction assembly and distortion screen holding rig for the GIMAS Lab Small Fan Distortion experiment. The test section interfaces upstream wind tunnel flow straighteners with a downstream ducted fan propulsor to evaluate propulsor response to distorted inflow profiles.

The geometry transforms a 12-inch square wind tunnel working section down to a 6-inch diameter circular duct. The contraction length was benchmarked against the MIT Gas Turbine Laboratory (GTL) 1x1 ft wind tunnel working section architecture to avoid boundary layer separation and adverse wall pressure gradients.

Transition Duct CAD Architecture

Square-to-Circular Contraction Loft Contoured transition duct featuring an upstream 12-inch flanged interface, smooth lofting to eliminate abrupt internal corners, and a 6-inch concentric circular collar interface for downstream duct insertion.
Collar & Screen Alignment Circular bore collar with pre-drilled bolt flanges to mount distortion/blank screens and secure test section piping with repeatable radial clocking.
Aerodynamic Architecture Reference Station layout adapted from the MIT GTL 1x1 ft wind tunnel standard, establishing Kiel probe stations, static taps, distortion screens, and a constant area duct length of L = 1.5 Dfan.

Distortion Screen Structural Validation (FEA)

A finite element analysis was conducted on the honeycomb flow-conditioning/distortion screen to verify structural integrity under maximum aerodynamic dynamic pressure loading.

von Mises Stress Distribution (σmax ≈ 18 MPa) Simulated under uniform aerodynamic drag loads. Peak stresses concentrate along outer perimeter cell junctions, remaining well within allowable yield margins for precision additive/subtractive tooling.

Material Selection Trade Study (Ducting)

The middle test section required a 6-inch nominal bore matching Schedule 40 dimensional tolerances (6.065-inch inner diameter, 6.625-inch outer diameter) while providing optical access for flow diagnostics.

Material Optical Clarity Thermal / Rigidity Availability & Cost Design Decision
Clear PVC (Sch 40) High (adequate for camera tracking) Good structural wall thickness (0.280") Off-the-shelf standard sizes; lowest cost Primary Choice: Exact fit for standard flanges
Extruded Acrylic Tube Excellent (near-glass clarity) Moderate; brittle under vibration Common commercial stock; moderate cost Backup Option: If optical PIV tracking requires higher clarity
Fused Quartz Superior optical transmittance Extreme temperature tolerance Custom fabrication only; highly expensive Rejected: Excessive cost; no high-heat requirement in fan duct
  • Standardization: Basing circular dimensions on standard Schedule 40 pipe avoided bespoke machining costs for circular coupling sleeves.
  • Modularity: Pre-drilled bolt flanges allow quick swapping between clean baseline screens, partial boundary layer blocks, and turbulent distortion grids.
